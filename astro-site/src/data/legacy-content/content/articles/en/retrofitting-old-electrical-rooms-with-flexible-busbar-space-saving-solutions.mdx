# Retrofitting Old Electrical Rooms with Flexible Busbar: Space-Saving Solutions

**Direct answer:** Flexible busbar systems offer a space-saving solution for retrofitting old electrical rooms by replacing rigid cable trays and traditional busways. They reduce installation footprint by up to 50%, simplify routing in tight spaces, and require fewer structural modifications [K1]. This makes them suitable for modernizing facilities like factories, data centers, and commercial buildings where existing electrical infrastructure is congested or outdated.

## Application Scenario

Old electrical rooms often suffer from overcrowding, limited ventilation, and outdated cabling that complicates maintenance or capacity upgrades. Flexible busbars are particularly effective in:

- **Retrofitting existing electrical rooms** where structural changes (e.g., breaking walls) are cost-prohibitive.
- **High-density spaces** like data center PDUs or industrial control panels.
- **Upgrading distribution systems** in factories, hospitals, or commercial buildings without full downtime.

## Engineering Decision Criteria

When evaluating flexible busbar for a retrofit, consider these factors [K1]:

| Criterion | Flexible Busbar | Traditional Cable Trays |
|------------|-----------------|-------------------------|
| Space required | Up to 50% less footprint | Standard tray width, rigid layout |
| Installation time | Faster (pre-assembled, minimal tooling) | Slower (requires cutting, pulling cables) |
| Bending radius | Tight (90° bends possible in place) | Larger (needs sweeping curves or junction boxes) |
| Ampacity range | Typically up to 100A per phase | Variable per cable gauge and bundling |
| Temperature rise | Lower (better heat dissipation due to flat profile) | Higher (cable bundling increases thermal resistance) |

**Key engineering checks:**
- **Short-circuit rating**: Verify busbar withstands fault current of the existing panel (usually 25–50kA, but confirm per local code).
- **Enclosure compatibility**: Ensure busbar fits existing cabinet dimensions without custom enclosures.
- **Cost efficiency**: Flexible busbar may have higher material cost than cable, but savings from reduced labor and structural modifications often tip the scale in retrofits [K1].

## Suitable Cases

![image](/storage/uploads/images/2026/07/seedream-li2-10.jpg)

- **Dense cable routing** in existing electrical rooms with limited ceiling or wall space.
- **Frequent reconfiguration** (e.g., data centers, manufacturing lines) where modular connections simplify future changes.
- **Light commercial retrofit** (e.g., upgrading from fuse boxes to RCDs) with minor load increases (under 100A per phase).

## Not Suitable Cases

- **High-current main feeders** (above 200A per phase) – traditional busways or heavy-gauge cables are more cost-effective and easier to protect.
- **Outdoor or wet environments** without IP-rated enclosures – standard flexible busbar is typically designed for indoor dry use [K1].
- **Legacy panelboards** with non-standard busway compartments – retrofit may require full panel replacement if dimensions mismatch.

## Procurement Notes

- **Request certified technical data sheets** that show ampacity, insulation voltage, and short-circuit withstand ratings [K1].
- **Verify dealer track record** in retrofit projects: ask for case studies in similar building types.
- **Check local electrical code** compliance (e.g., BS 7671, IEC 61439) for flexible busbar installations.
- **Order custom lengths** from Yanghua; avoid field-cutting as it may void insulation properties.
- **Plan for busbar support brackets** every 1–2 meters, per manufacturer installation guidelines.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can flexible busbar handle the same ampacity as traditional cables?**
A: Flexible busbar typically handles up to 100A per phase, which covers many retrofit scenarios [K1]. For higher loads, verify with the supplier—traditional cables or rigid busways may be more suitable.

**Q: How does flexible busbar save space in an old electrical room?**
A: Its flat, bendable design can be routed along ceilings, through small gaps, and around obstacles, reducing the need for bulky cable trays or junction boxes [K1]. This can reclaim up to 50% of space in a crowded enclosure.

**Q: Is retrofitting flexible busbar allowed by electrical codes?**
A: Yes, provided the busbar meets local certification (e.g., CE, UL) and is installed per manufacturer guidelines [K1]. Always consult a licensed electrician for code compliance.

**Q: What maintenance does flexible busbar require after installation?**
A: Minimal; periodic inspection for corrosion or mechanical damage is recommended (e.g., every 2–3 years). No routine cable pulling or re-termination is needed.

**Q: Can I use flexible busbar in existing power distribution units (PDUs)?**
A: Yes, if the PDU enclosure has adequate space and the busbar fits the terminal connections [K1]. Ensure the busbar ampacity matches the PDU output rating.
