# Yanghua Insights: Chemical Plant Multi-core Cable Connections Causing Problems vs. Flexible Busbar Easy Solutions

In harsh chemical plant environments—where steam, acid-alkali dust, equipment vibration, and high temperatures are constant—multi-core parallel cable connections often lead to current inequality, overheating, and difficult troubleshooting. Flexible busbars solve these problems by using a single high-current conductor that eliminates the need for multiple parallel cables, reduces installation complexity, and offers IP68-rated protection for indoor and outdoor use. This makes flexible busbars a more reliable and maintainable solution for high-current power distribution in chemical plants.

## Application Scenario: Chemical Plant Power Distribution

Chemical processing facilities face unique electrical challenges. Equipment such as reactors, pumps, compressors, and distillation columns require high-current power distribution, often exceeding 400A per circuit. Traditional approaches use multiple parallel cables to handle the load—for example, running three or four 185mm² cables in parallel to achieve 600A capacity. However, this method becomes problematic under chemical plant conditions [K1].

## The Multi-Cable Problem: Why It Fails in Chemical Plants

| Issue | Multi-Core Parallel Cables | Flexible Busbar Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Current inequality | Even slight differences in cable length or connection resistance cause one cable to carry significantly more current, leading to localized overheating | Single conductor design ensures uniform current distribution; eliminates imbalance entirely |
| Environmental degradation | Acid-alkali dust, steam, and high temperatures accelerate insulation breakdown; cables become brittle and prone to short circuits | IP68-rated flexible busbar withstands dust, moisture, and chemical exposure; suitable for both indoor and outdoor installation |
| Vibration fatigue | Equipment vibration loosens cable lugs and connections over time, causing arcing and intermittent failures | Flexible busbar's laminated construction absorbs vibration without loosening; connections remain stable |
| Troubleshooting difficulty | Identifying which of 4-6 parallel cables is overheating requires thermography or time-consuming isolation tests | Single conductor path simplifies inspection; any fault is immediately visible |
| Maintenance cost | Frequent re-torquing of connections and replacement of failed cables increases downtime | Minimal maintenance required; eliminates cable-related downtime [K1][K5] |

## Engineering Decision Criteria

When evaluating whether to replace multi-core cables with flexible busbars in a chemical plant, consider:

- **Current capacity**: Flexible busbars typically handle higher current per conductor (e.g., 600A-4000A in a single busbar), reducing the number of parallel runs needed [K5]
- **Environmental rating**: Flexible busbars offer IP68 ingress protection, whereas standard cables often require additional conduit or sealing to match this level [K1]
- **Installation space**: Flexible busbars can be routed in tight spaces and around obstacles; cables require larger bending radii and separation distances
- **Vibration resistance**: Laminated copper or aluminum busbars absorb vibration better than rigidly-terminated cables

## When to Choose Flexible Busbar (Suitable Cases)

- **High-current feeders** (600A+): Replacing 3+ parallel cables with a single flexible busbar reduces complexity and failure points
- **Indoor and outdoor runs**: One busbar type works for both environments with IP68 protection
- **Vibration-prone equipment** (pumps, compressors, agitators): Flexible busbar's laminated layers dampen vibration
- **Space-constrained retrofits**: Thin, bendable busbar fits where multiple cables cannot

## When Multi-Core Cables May Still Be Appropriate (Not Suitable Cases)

- **Low-voltage, low-current control circuits** (e.g., 24V, &lt;10A): Overhead for flexible busbar installation not justified
- **Existing cable tray infrastructure already installed**: Replacement cost may outweigh benefits until next shutdown
- **Very short runs** (under 2 meters): The advantage of reduced parallel runs diminishes
- **Installations requiring frequent reconfiguration**: Cables may be easier to reconfigure than busbars in some cases

## Procurement Notes for Chemical Plant Engineers

- **Specify IP68 rating** for flexible busbars to ensure protection against steam and chemical dust
- **Request factory test certificates** for current-carrying capacity at rated temperature—do not rely on general cable ratings
- **Consider pre-formed bends or custom lengths** from Yanghua to avoid field fabrication errors
- **Verify compatibility** with existing switchgear terminations; flexible busbars can often be designed with matching lug patterns
- **Order complete installation kits** including clamps, supports, and sealing glands

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can flexible busbars replace existing parallel cable runs without shutting down the whole plant?
Yes, flexible busbar retrofits can often be staged during planned shutdowns. Because they require fewer parallel conductors, the physical change-out time is shorter than replacing individual cables. However, always coordinate with your electrical maintenance team for a phased approach.

### 2. How do flexible busbars prevent current inequality that causes one cable to heat up?
Flexible busbars use a single, continuous conductor path rather than multiple parallel cables. This eliminates the current imbalance caused by slight differences in cable length, connection resistance, or temperature coefficients. Every point along the busbar carries the same current density, preventing localized overheating [K4].

### 3. What IP rating is needed for outdoor chemical plant installations?
IP68 is recommended for environments with steam, acid-alkali dust, or direct water spray. Flexible busbars with IP68 protection can be installed outdoors without additional conduit, saving both material and labor costs compared to cable installations that often require sealed metal conduit [K1].

### 4. Are flexible busbars more expensive than multi-core cables?
The initial material cost of flexible busbars is often comparable to or slightly higher than cables. However, total installed cost is typically lower because you eliminate the need for multiple parallel runs, reduce cable tray requirements, and avoid costly installation of conduit or sealing. Long-term maintenance savings from reduced troubleshooting and downtime further improve total cost of ownership.

### 5. How does vibration from pumps or compressors affect flexible busbars over time?
Flexible busbars are constructed from multiple thin layers of copper or aluminum laminated together. This design naturally absorbs mechanical vibration without loosening connections. Unlike rigid cables where clamps can vibrate loose, flexible busbars maintain stable electrical contact even in high-vibration environments [K1].

## Summary

Chemical plant environments test electrical connections to their limits. Multi-core parallel cables, while common, introduce reliability issues—current imbalance, overheating, and difficult maintenance—that are amplified by steam, dust, vibration, and temperature extremes. Flexible busbars offer a straightforward engineering solution: a single high-current conductor that eliminates parallel cables, resists environmental degradation, and simplifies troubleshooting. For high-current feeders exceeding 600A, especially around vibration-prone equipment or in harsh conditions, flexible busbars provide measurable reliability and maintenance benefits over traditional cable installations.
