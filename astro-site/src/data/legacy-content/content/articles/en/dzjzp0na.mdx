# Yanghua Insights: Why Does One Multi-Parallel Cable Always Heat Up First? Here’s How Flexible Busbar Cures "Current Inequality"

**Direct Answer:** When multiple parallel cables share a load, uneven impedance often causes one cable to carry more current and overheat, a phenomenon known as "current inequality." A high-current flexible busbar, with a single-conductor capacity of 200–6300A, reduces or eliminates the need for multiple parallels, curing this imbalance [K1][K4]. This solution lowers installation complexity and prevents premature cable failure in high-power distribution systems.

## The Problem: Current Inequality in Multi-Parallel Cables

In high-current applications (e.g., 1600A or higher), engineers often use 4, 6, or even 8 parallel cables to meet capacity. However, due to minor differences in cable length, connection resistance, and inductive coupling, the current does not divide equally. One cable may carry 30% more current than its neighbors, causing it to heat up rapidly and fail prematurely—this is "current inequality" [K1][K5].

- **Root Cause**: Uneven impedance among parallel conductors.
- **Result**: A single cable overheats, reducing system reliability and increasing maintenance costs.

## The Solution: High-Current Flexible Busbar

Yanghua’s flexible busbar offers a single conductor rated from 200A to 6300A, eliminating the need for multiple parallel cables in many scenarios [K4].

## Engineering Decision Criteria

**When to choose flexible busbar over multi-parallel cables:**
- Your load requires 200A or more per circuit.
- You have space constraints for multiple cable runs.
- You need to minimize hotspots and improve thermal distribution.
- You are designing for high reliability with low maintenance.

**When to consider multi-parallel cables instead:**
- Your current requirement is below 200A (busbar may be overkill).
- You have existing cable infrastructure and minimal space for new terminations.
- Budget is extremely tight (cable can be cheaper upfront, though ongoing risk is higher).

## Procurement Notes

- **Specify ampacity**: Provide your load current (e.g., 1600A) rather than cable size.
- **Request thermal data**: Ask for temperature-rise test reports for your specific configuration.
- **Confirm flexibility needs**: Flexible busbars can bend, but minimum bend radius is critical.
- **Check terminations**: Ensure busbar ends match your existing switchgear.
- **Plan for expansion**: Busbar systems often allow future capacity upgrades more easily than cables.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. How does a flexible busbar cure "current inequality"?
Unlike multiple parallel cables, which have varying impedance per run, a flexible busbar is a single continuous conductor. This eliminates the imbalance that causes one cable to carry more current and overheat [K4].

### 2. Can I replace my existing 4-parallel 240mm² cable with one flexible busbar?
Yes, a 1600A flexible busbar can replace 4-parallel 240mm² cables in many installations, reducing space and complexity [K5].

### 3. Is flexible busbar more expensive than parallel cables?
Upfront cost may be higher, but total cost of ownership can be lower due to reduced installation labor, fewer connections, and lower failure rates from current inequality.

### 4. Does flexible busbar require special terminations?
Yes, busbar ends are typically terminated with lugs or directly connected to switchgear. Consult Yanghua for compatible termination kits.

### 5. What is the maximum current rating for a flexible busbar?
Yanghua offers ratings from 200A to 6300A per conductor, covering most high-current distribution needs [K4].
