# Yanghua Insights: High Current Multi-core Cables Requiring 4 or Even 8 Parallel Connections? 1600A Flexible Busbar VS 240mm² Cable 4-Parallel

For high-current power distribution demanding 1600A or more, using 4-parallel 240mm² cables introduces significant engineering risks including current imbalance, uneven heating, and installation complexity. A single 1600A flexible busbar offers a simpler, more reliable alternative by eliminating parallel connections, reducing voltage drop, and ensuring uniform current distribution across the conductor cross-section. This comparison guides engineers evaluating cable parallelism versus busbar replacement for high-current applications. [K1][K2]

## Application Scenario

High-current power distribution systems in industrial plants, data centers, renewable energy installations, and large commercial buildings often require ampacity ratings of 1000A to 1600A or higher. Traditional cable solutions typically involve running multiple cables in parallel—for instance, four 240mm² cables to achieve 1600A capacity. These parallel cable configurations are common in:

- Main power distribution panels and switchgear connections
- Transformer-to-main-distribution-board (MDB) links
- Large motor control centers (MCCs)
- UPS output and battery room connections
- Solar farm inverter-to-transformer interconnections

However, engineers increasingly question whether parallel cables are the most reliable and cost-effective solution for high-current runs. [K1][K3]

## Engineering Decision Criteria

### Current Imbalance and Heating

The core engineering concern with multi-parallel cables is current inequality. Even with identical cable types and lengths, slight differences in contact resistance, cable routing, and termination impedance cause one cable to carry disproportionately more current. This "hottest leg" heats up faster, increasing resistance further and accelerating thermal runaway. Source material confirms that multi-parallel cables "always have one heating up first." [K5]

### Installation Complexity and Space

Four 240mm² cables require significantly more installation space, tray width, and bend radius compared to a single flexible busbar. Terminating four cables also demands more lug connections, torque checks, and inspection points—each a potential failure site.

### Voltage Drop and Efficiency

A single flexible busbar with lower inductance and resistance per amp reduces overall voltage drop compared to parallel cables, improving system efficiency and reducing power loss over long runs.

## Comparison: 1600A Flexible Busbar vs. 240mm² Cable 4-Parallel

| Parameter | 240mm² Cable 4-Parallel | 1600A Flexible Busbar |
|---|---|---|
| **Ampacity** | ~1600A (derated for grouping) | 1600A (rated) |
| **Number of conductors** | 4 separate cables + grounding | 1 busbar |
| **Current balance** | High risk of imbalance (10-30% deviation) | Near-uniform distribution |
| **Installation time** | 2-3x longer (multiple pulls, terminations) | Single-run installation |
| **Space requirement** | ~4x cable tray width | 1x busbar width |
| **Failure points** | 4x terminations, 4x cable joints | Single termination per end |
| **Flexibility** | Poor (bend radius 8-12x cable diameter) | Excellent (tight bend radius) |

## Suitable Cases for Flexible Busbar

- **High-reliability systems:** UPS, data centers, hospitals where downtime is unacceptable
- **Space-constrained installations:** Retrofits, equipment rooms, cable trays already at capacity
- **Long-distance runs:** Where voltage drop and power loss are critical
- **Complex routing:** Multiple bends, tight spaces, or difficult access
- **New high-current designs:** Specifying 1600A or above, especially with limited available ampacity

## Not Suitable Cases

- **Existing parallel cable installations with no performance issues:** If the installation operates reliably and imbalance is within limits, replacement may not be justified
- **Very short runs** (under 10 meters): The efficiency and installation advantages diminish
- **When voltage drop is already acceptable:** If system voltage drop is within design limits, the busbar's electrical advantage may not offset cost
- **Where cable inventory already exists:** Standard cables already purchased or in stock may be more economical than switching to busbar

## Procurement Notes

- Verify ampacity ratings with the manufacturer for specific installation conditions (ambient temperature, grouping, altitude)
- Request current distribution test data or simulation results from flexible busbar suppliers
- For flexible busbar, confirm bend radius and minimum clearance requirements per manufacturer
- Always specify the exact current requirements and any future expansion plans
- Compare total installed cost (TIC) including cable, tray, terminations, labor, and maintenance—not just material price

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: Why do parallel cables have current imbalance?**

Current imbalance occurs because even identical cables have slight differences in contact resistance at terminations, twisting or routing effects that change impedance, and manufacturing tolerances. This causes one cable to carry more current, heat up faster, and increase its resistance further—a positive feedback loop that accelerates uneven wear and failure. [K5]

**Q2: Can I replace existing 4-parallel 240mm² cables with a single 1600A flexible busbar without redesigning the system?**

Generally yes, but a site-specific evaluation is required. You need to verify that the existing terminations, enclosure space, and routing can accommodate the flexible busbar dimensions. The busbar also requires different connector types than cable lugs. Consult the manufacturer's application engineering team for a compatibility assessment. [K1]

**Q3: What is the maximum current rating available for flexible busbars?**

Flexible busbars are available from various manufacturers in ratings from a few hundred amps up to 6300A or more. For 1600A applications, a single busbar or a stacked busbar configuration can typically meet the requirement. Always check the manufacturer's specific ampacity tables for your installation conditions. [K1]

**Q4: How does installation cost compare between parallel cables and flexible busbars?**

Total installed cost (TIC) for flexible busbar is often lower or comparable to parallel cables when considering labor time (single installation vs. four pulls), material (one busbar vs. four cables plus lugs), and reduced inspection/maintenance. However, material cost per amp may initially appear higher for busbar. A complete TIC analysis including tray width, supports, and terminations is recommended. [K1]

**Q5: Do flexible busbars require special training for installation?**

Installation of flexible busbars is similar to installing standard cables but requires awareness of appropriate bend radii and termination torque specifications. Most manufacturers provide installation guides and training. No advanced or specialized electrical license beyond standard electrician certification is typically required. [K2]
